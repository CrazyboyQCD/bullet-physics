# Deviations from Bullet

This document tracks every place where this crate deliberately differs from
Bullet's behavior, is numerically different, or has not yet been implemented.
It is the canonical reference for "does X match Bullet?" — checked against
`refs/bullet3` source on 2026-08-13 and re-verified subsystem-by-subsystem in
the rolling semantic audit (contact/manifold/solver core + solver-info defaults
2026-09-01, CCD sweep / Vehicle / Character / joint limit-motors / soft-body
solvers 2026-09-01, see §4).

## 1. Intentional deviations (kept)

| Area | This crate | Bullet | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capsule/Cylinder/Cone local inertia | Exact analytic formulas | AABB-box approximation | More accurate, cheap to compute |
| Ray cast from inside a shape | Reports `fraction = 0` with `normal = ray direction` | Misses (the `n · r` check rejects interior starts) | Mainstream-engine semantics; documented in `raycast.rs` |
| Dynamic (GImpact-style) mesh bodies | One `TriangleMesh` shape serves both uses: static bodies behave like `btBvhTriangleMeshShape`, dynamic bodies are Bullet's GImpact case (vertex point-mass inertia, mesh-vs-mesh and plane-vs-mesh contact paths) | Two classes: `btBvhTriangleMeshShape` (static) and `btGImpactMeshShape` (dynamic) | The data (soup + local-frame BVH, never rebuilt for rigid motion) is identical; the calling convention replaces the class split. Heightfields stay static-only |
| Mesh-vs-mesh triangle collision | Generic GJK+EPA per candidate triangle pair (the shape of Bullet's `BULLET_TRIANGLE_COLLISION` build; prebuilt triangle hulls reused) | Default build clips triangle polygons (`collide_sat_triangles` + `find_triangle_collision_clip_method`), producing contact polygons | Architectural reuse: the same per-triangle pipeline as the convex-vs-mesh path. Three structural consequences: (a) exactly co-planar overlaps and line-crossing contacts have a zero-volume Minkowski difference where EPA cannot resolve a normal (resting meshes live in the separated band and are unaffected); (b) single-sided zero-thickness plates can tunnel when one step drives them deeper than the pair band — closed shells are covered by their far-side triangles; (c) gimpact *stacks* are unstable on both engines — a 5x5x4 shell block (0.02 gaps, 12-triangle closed shells) blows up on Bullet around frame 20 and its shells end up tunneled through the ground, while ours rides the margin band and slumps to half height without tunneling (2026-08-31 `--dump` comparison, both perf runners) — dynamic concave stacking is a Bullet weak spot too, not a unique single-point-manifold artifact; use convex decomposition or rigid stacks where stack integrity matters |
| Mesh-vs-mesh contact band | Margin-shell semantics: triangle pairs closer than `max(pair threshold, 0.1)` report `penetration = band − signed_separation` (Bullet's clip-method `margin − distance`), so the solver holds meshes apart positionally; resting shells hover `band − slop` above the surface | Margin sum of the two primitives (0.04 + 0.04 by default); positive-distance points are velocity-only, penetration-only points push positionally — meshes settle near the surface | A velocity-only band cannot stop a falling zero-thickness mesh: past the surface the separation only grows, so there is nothing to recover from. Probe-classified: resting ~4.4 cm higher than Bullet on a flat BVH ground (`shell_on_bvh`); a one-step tunnel (deeper than the band) can also land the pair past the surface, where GJK's unsigned distance picks the flipped normal — keep per-step travel below the band (probe scenes use slow drops) |
| Plane-vs-mesh contacts | Per-vertex distances against the fixed plane normal (single-sided, like Bullet's `gimpacttrimeshpart_vs_plane_collision`), gated by the pair contact band | Same per-vertex path, gated by the shape margins | No toward-mesh reorientation: for a zero-thickness mesh that would flip the normal right as the body crosses the plane and drop the support. Note the convex-vs-plane path *does* reorient (shapes with thickness survive the crossing) |
| Friction warm starting | Not inherited across steps (rebuilds from zero each step) | Inherited (`m_appliedImpulseLateral1/2`), `m_warmstartingFactor = 0.85` | The tangent basis is rebuilt from the current normal every step; on triangle meshes it rotates with the point set, and stale friction kicks made resting bodies oscillate (`falling_mesh`). Normal-impulse warm starting IS implemented |
| Contact manifold persistence | Per-pair persistent manifolds (`PersistentManifold`): points anchored in both local frames, re-projected without re-running detection (`refreshContactPoints` equivalent), merge-on-capture (`addManifoldPoint` semantics: matched points replace and inherit the normal impulse, new points append, undetected points survive until the refresh prunes them; capacity 4, deepest survive on overflow), entries pruned when the broad-phase pair dies | Full `btPersistentManifold` with point lifetime counters, friction anchors, contact-processed callbacks | The merge semantics (the load-bearing part) are aligned as of 2026-09-01; before that, capture replaced the whole cached set, a resting flat box lived on a single flipping corner point, and the solver torque pumped it through the plane (the wide-flat-box bug) — a divergence the old wording of this row ("`refreshContactPoints` equivalent") failed to flag. Still missing: point lifetime counters, friction anchors, contact-processed callbacks. The anchored refresh skips the narrow phase for slow pairs (the perf win); see the next row for the guards |
| Persistent manifold cache: refresh guards | A pair re-detects when its end-of-last-step speed exceeds 0.08 m/s *and* the cache would serve a point deeper than the slop (Baumgarte-biased); margin-band points (separation within `gContactBreakThreshold`) are served at any speed as velocity-only contacts; a 5 mm relative-pose drift also forces re-detection | Points are re-detected every step, so their depth/normal are always fresh | A stale anchored depth feeding the Baumgarte bias is an energy pump that blows up settling stacks (reproduced on the 10x10x10 perf stack: max velocity crept 0.4 → 10 m/s over ~200 frames); the guards bound the staleness to the no-bias regime. Resting heights shift ~5 mm per interface vs both Bullet and the pre-cache build (margin-band support, see the stacking parity classification) |
| `activate()` on disabled bodies | Skips `DisableDeactivation` and `DisableSimulation` bodies (they keep their state) | Forces `ACTIVE_TAG` — only the static/kinematic collision flags are checked | Preserving `DISABLE_DEACTIVATION`/`DISABLE_SIMULATION` semantics is safer for the common "never sleep / never simulate" usage |
| Body removal: constraint cascade | `PhysicsWorld::remove_body` removes every constraint referencing the body (joints, multibody-vs-rigidbody constraints, their collision suppression) and panics on double removal | `removeRigidBody` leaves the constraints dangling — callers must remove them first or the pointers go stale | Index-based references cannot dangle silently; removing them up-front is what Bullet's own demos do. Body slots are stable (never reused, other indices unchanged) like Bullet's pointer identity; accessing a removed body panics |
| Broad phase | `AxisSweep3` (three-axis sweep-and-prune) as the default; `DbvtBroadPhase` (dynamic BVH, `btDbvtBroadphase`'s port) selectable via `BroadPhaseBackend`/`PhysicsWorld::with_broadphase` | `btDbvtBroadphase` (dynamic BVH tree) is Bullet's default; `btAxisSweep3` is opt-in | Sweep-and-prune with incremental sorted lists is simpler and fast for moderate scenes; the Dbvt port exists for very large dynamic scenes (both trees, fat volumes, stage migration and per-step rotation match Bullet — see `broadphase::dbvt`) |
| Contact detection pose | Predicted pose (`position + v·dt` after velocity integration); no `createPredictiveContacts` stage — every moving pair is detected at the predicted pose instead | Current (previous-step) pose (`m_worldTransform`, detection runs before `integrateTransforms`) | Contacts are detected one step earlier on impact; transient impact frames differ by one step, rest states still converge. Measured in the `falling_box` parity scene: 0.155 m peak on the impact frame, within tolerance from the next frame on |
| Margin-band contacts | GJK distance walk reports contacts while surfaces are separated by up to the per-pair threshold `min(disc_a, disc_b) · 0.02` (disc = bounding-sphere radius + center offset of the *true* local AABB); plane/sphere-box fast paths do the same; sphere-sphere and box-box SAT stay penetration-only (matching Bullet's algorithm split); broad-phase AABBs padded by the global 0.02; solver adds Bullet's separation-rate softening for separated points | Same band semantics, but discs are measured on margin-inflated AABBs and the GJK walks run on margin-shrunk cores (`m_maximumDistanceSquared = marginA + marginB + breaking`) | Our shapes carry no margin, so the band is a few percent narrower (e.g. 17.3 vs 18.7 mm for a 1 m box/sphere pair) and the core/witness geometry is exact; the combined predicted-pose + band offset makes the first plane-contact frame precede Bullet's by one to two steps at ~9 m/s (falling_capsule transient, classified). Band points have negative `penetration` and are velocity-only (see the slop row in §2) |
| Friction directions | Two orthogonal tangent directions per contact | One velocity-dependent direction by default (`SOLVER_USE_2_FRICTION_DIRECTIONS` is off) | Slightly different friction response on asymmetric contacts; documented trade-off |
| Rigid-body gyroscopic force | Not implemented: angular velocity is constant while a body is airborne (no contact), so the *angular velocity* is what stays fixed | Default-on (`m_rigidbodyFlags = BT_ENABLE_GYROSCOPIC_FORCE_IMPLICIT_BODY`, applied per step in `btSequentialImpulseConstraintSolver::convertBodies`, L1386-96): an implicit gyroscopic impulse `dt·(ω × I₁ω)` in body coordinates keeps the *angular momentum* approximately conserved | Discovered in the 2026-09-01 semantic audit. Any airborne tumbling of a non-spherical body (long/flat shapes above all) follows a different flip path: Bullet shows real gyroscopic precession, we keep ω constant. Symmetric bodies and rest/contact-dominated motion are unaffected; all parity scenes fall into the latter class, which is why no test flagged it. Multibodies are unaffected either way (Featherstone ABA carries the gyroscopic term natively). Candidate future work: implicit-body gyroscopic impulse in `integrate` |
| Kinematic pose driving (2026-09-04) | The pose integrates from the user-set velocities every step (gravity/damping/forces never touch them); `new_kinematic` starts `DisableDeactivation` | Kinematics are driven by the user/motion-state setting the transform (the velocity only feeds contact relative velocities) and walk the normal sleep state machine | A library without motion states needs the velocity-driven mode (it is the only automatic pose source); a per-frame `set_translation` composes with it exactly like Bullet's transform-driven mode. Without `DisableDeactivation` the sleep state machine would stall the velocity-driven pose |
| CCD sweep: allowed penetration & direction guard | The swept-sphere cast reports exact geometric entry (`swept_sphere_cast`); no `m_allowedCcdPenetration` slack and no result-level `hitNormal·v >= -allowed` reject — a body that *starts* inside a target and moves away still clamps at fraction 0 | `btClosestNotMeConvexResultCallback::addSingleResult` rejects hits whose normal does not oppose the motion within `m_allowedCcdPenetration = 0.04` (`btDispatcherInfo` default), and the plane caster loosens its hit distance by the same amount, so Bullet's clamps land up to ~4 cm early | Found in the 2026-09-01 semantic audit (source-level; the `ccd` parity scene's 0.075 mm clamp/rest agreement already shows the difference does not dominate the recorded scenario). Porting the 0.04 slack would shift every CCD clamp landing and require re-recording the parity scene — candidate follow-up if CCD rest precision matters |
| CCD clamp: which motion is rewound | Only the position is rewound by the hit fraction; the orientation integrates the full step | `predictIntegratedTransform(dt · hitFraction)` re-predicts the *whole* transform, so the rotation is scaled by the fraction too | Same 2026-09-01 audit; only visible for fast bodies with significant spin during the clamped step |
| CCD sweep: compound bodies | A fast compound body is swept when every child is convex (`CollisionShape::is_convex` asks GJK-usability, which all-convex compounds satisfy); a compound *target* is hit via its aggregate support (distance to the convex hull of the children) | `btCollisionShape::isConvex()` is the enum-range check `proxyType < CONCAVE_SHAPES_START_HERE` — compound shapes are NOT convex, so a fast compound body is never swept; as sweep targets, compounds descend per child shape | 2026-09-01 audit; the difference is conservative on our side (we sweep more often, never less) |
| Soft body: `static_solve` scope | Refine's relaxation loop runs the position sequence *minus* soft-soft contacts | `btSoftBody::staticSolve` runs the full `m_psequence`, soft-soft contacts included | Found in the 2026-09-01 audit; only observable when a body has live soft-soft contacts while being refined (a modeling-time API, no probe scene) |
| Solver-info defaults | Fully audited against `btContactSolverInfo` (2026-09-01): erp 0.2 / erp2 0.2 / splitImpulse true / split threshold −0.04 / turnErp 0.1 / numIterations 10 / warmstartingFactor 0.85 / sor 1.0 / minimumSolverBatchSize 128 all match; combined friction is the product with a ±10 clamp and combined restitution is max (both match); rigid-body default friction 0.5 and restitution 0 match `btCollisionObject`'s constructor | Same defaults, with the exceptions in §2 (slop 0 vs 0.005, restitution velocity threshold 0.2 vs 1.0) | Mechanical cross-check requested by the semantic-audit convention: default-value drift is invisible to per-scene probes when scenes set parameters explicitly. Not applicable / unused here: `m_maxErrorReduction` and `m_tau`/`m_damping` only feed soft bodies and the unported Spring2 constraints; `m_numNonContactInnerIterations` has no row-level counterpart (our multibody constraints join the single PGS loop; motor probes are within tolerance); `m_globalCfm`/`m_frictionCFM` are 0 and unused |
| Vehicle: axle extraction | Wheel-friction axle is the right-axis **column** of the wheel basis (`-basis.col(right_axis)`, spin-invariant) | Right-axis **row** (`-basis.row(right_axis)`), which picks up a `cos(rotation)` factor from the accumulated wheel spin | The row extraction makes the steering direction oscillate at the wheel-spin frequency — the net turning washes out and a steered car drives straight with a small wobble (reproduced with the literal port). The column is the same direction for an unspun wheel and stays correct as the wheel rolls |
| Vehicle: wheel raycast vs. chassis | Wheel rays explicitly exclude the chassis body | The ray may start inside the chassis; Bullet's interior-start rays miss (the `n · r` check), so the chassis never hits | We report interior-start rays (see above), so the exclusion is explicit here; same effective behavior |
| Vehicle: ground object | The ground is always the fixed (static) body; the hit body index is stored in `WheelInfo::raycast_info.ground_body` but no impulse is applied to it | Same (`m_groundObject = &getFixedBody()`, with a `@todo` for dynamic ground) | Aligned with Bullet: driving on dynamic objects is not supported |
| Islands: node space | One union-find node per multibody (the collider chain is connected by construction) | One node per link/base collider, merged along the adjacent-collider chain | Equivalent partitions with less bookkeeping; also lets collider-less multibody constraints wake a sleeping body (tested semantics). Full list in `docs/multibody-collision.md` §9.8 |
| Islands: solving | One global solve batch; islands only filter which rows exist | Per-island batches, accumulated until `m_minimumSolverBatchSize = 128` rows | Below 128 rows Bullet also solves everything as one group, so this matches for typical scenes; row order is unchanged (parity-stable). Splitting batches is a later perf item |
| Islands: sleeping pair detection | Pairs of a sleeping island skip the narrow phase entirely | Collision detection still runs for sleeping pairs (only solving is skipped) | With the per-step impulse cache this only affects warm-start freshness after a wake (one-frame cold-start transient) |
| Soft body: node-vs-rigid distance | Exact distance: analytic planes, the GJK distance walk with a zero-radius sphere for convex shapes (margin-inflated band `distance − margin < 0`), closest-point-on-triangle over the BVH for static meshes/heightfields | `btSparseSdf` voxel cache: GJK point distances sampled on 0.25-voxel cell grids, trilinearly interpolated (planes never enter the GJK branch directly — `isConvex(plane)` is false; the concave algorithm triangulates the plane instead, stacking a third approximation) | The interpolation error and the triangle/extrusion route disappear; probe-quantified (2026-08-31, `docs/bullet-compare.md` "SoftBody probes"): free fall and pure link PBD match to print precision, but bodies settle **85–86 mm lower than Bullet** on planes — Bullet's stacked approximations open its effective contact band above the geometric margin, while we settle at `margin + 3 mm`. Same offset in f32 and f64 → structural; there is no exact Bullet reference for soft-vs-plane contacts to align to. Re-measured on a wavy static-mesh terrain (perf scene `softbody_mesh`, 2026-09-01): the same ~85 mm offset over the troughs — structural across static shape types, not a plane artifact. Cost side (see `docs/bullet-perf.md`): on meshes the exact per-node BVH query is stateless and dominates the step (86%), i.e. *more expensive* than Bullet's amortized cache — the end-to-end comparison still favors us (0.39x vs 0.23x on the plane), and a cross-frame query cache is the follow-up candidate if mesh-heavy soft workloads matter |
| Soft body: sleeping | Soft bodies never sleep (their node speed bookkeeping exists but the pipeline never consults it) | Same — `updateActivationState` only walks the rigid list; a soft body keeps its touching rigid islands awake | Aligned with Bullet's actual behavior (deviation from the docs, not the code) |
| Soft body: cluster-vs-concave | `CL_RS` supports convex rigid shapes and planes (the plane distance is the hull's extreme vertex — exact); static triangle meshes and heightfields produce no cluster contacts | Bullet routes cluster-vs-concave through `btSoftBodyConcaveCollisionAlgorithm`: the mesh is triangulated per triangle into extruded hulls (±0.04) and each runs the cluster GJK | Concave grounds with cluster bodies need a convex stand-in (a plane or a box); recorded gap, to be filled by per-triangle hull queries if a scene needs it |
| Soft body: cluster contact friction | The `CJoint` friction term applies only while the contact approaches (`rvac < 0`), so a sliding/rolling cluster body keeps its tangential speed | Same — `CJoint::Solve` gates the friction on `rvac < 0` (btSoftBody.cpp 3706) | Verbatim port of a Bullet quirk: cluster bodies glide indefinitely on planes (probe `cluster_pair` shows both engines doing it); documented so it is not "fixed" into a behavior difference |
| Soft body: SDF_RS band capacity | Same per-frame partial correction as Bullet (the position solve applies `kst = 1/piterations` of the velocity correction plus the `kKHR/kCHR`-scaled depth push; against a static partner `kKHR = 0.1`) | Same — `PSolve_RContacts` is a verbatim port including the `impulse.length() * 0.5` writeback quirk | A short rope dropped onto a curved convex (sphere) at ~4 m/s **tunnels through in both engines** (probe `rope_ball_tunnel` fast-drop variant: Bullet min_y −186.8 vs ours −181.0 over 400 frames; at zero drop height inside the band Bullet still tunnels): the per-frame correction cannot keep up with a single node supporting the whole rope, and the band edge turns into a catch-leak limit cycle. Our exact-distance band is narrower than Bullet's interpolated one, so the leak starts a few frames earlier — a capacity limit of the classic system, not a port bug. A draped rope (band-internal start, many nodes sharing the load) is held ~30 frames then slides off — both engines agree within a few frames of phase slip |
| Soft body: quickhull vertex order | Hull vertices are the input indices in ascending order (deterministic subset) | `HullLibrary` re-indexes and merges its output vertices | Same vertex set, different node/link/face append order → different PBD solve order → settle scatter; probe `ellipsoid_ground` diverges ~1e-4 while still airborne (frame 22) — the pre-recorded scatter class, quantified |
| Soft body: rigid-fit rotation in `transform_to` | The Kabsch rotation comes from the polar decomposition of the rest-vs-current covariance (`R = Wᵀ`, reusing the cluster `polar_decompose`) | `getRigidTransform` runs `btImplicitQRSVD` and returns `V·Uᵀ` | Identical for full-rank covariances (the polar factor *is* `U·Vᵀ`); rank-deficient fits (degenerate/flattened bodies) may pick a different valid rotation. Transform-API-only path — no solver impact |
| Soft body: `refine`/`cutLink` on 1-D topology | Chain topology after a signed cut is fully culled by the orphan cleanup (every chain end has rank 1) | Same — Bullet's `refine(cut=true)` re-routes links whose *both* ends evaluate below the accuracy (the signed test covers the whole negative half-space), then removes rank-1 links | Verbatim port; refine/cut is a surface-mesh tool and produces dangling chains on ropes. The `todetach == 0` sentinel quirk (link index 0 never re-routes through the in-place branch) is also ported verbatim |
| Multibody joint feedback | Value slot per link (`enable_link_joint_feedback` / `link_joint_feedback`), frame flags on the `MultiBody` (`set_joint_feedback_in_world_space` / `..._in_joint_frame`) | An externally owned `btMultiBodyJointFeedback` struct assigned to `m_jointFeedback`, frame flags in the global `btContactSolverInfo` | Ownership-safe Rust API; the reported wrench (`top` = linear force, `bottom` = angular torque, link frame by default) and the two-pass `=`/`+=` write semantics match Bullet exactly (bit-identical free-swing probe, `docs/bullet-compare.md` "Multibody joint-feedback probes") |
| Multibody-vs-rigidbody constraint rows: gravity at assembly | Rows assemble from the gravity-integrated real velocity (our engine integrates gravity before the solve) | `fillMultiBodyConstraint` reads `rb->getLinearVelocity()` *without* the current gravity step (its rigid-rigid joint rows do add `m_externalForceImpulse`); gravity is injected only at the writeback and the leftover decays ~20%/frame | Bullet's mb↔rb rows leak one gravity step per frame (~1.36 cm sag on a welded ball, measured f32/f64) and erase initial velocities without a trace; our rows see the full velocity and hold the weld exactly. | Ghost overlap list (2026-09-07) | `PhysicsWorld::overlapping(handle)` reads the pair pass's persistent touch state: entries are narrow-phase contacts (an actual manifold inside the breaking threshold), maintained for sensor pairs (either side `NO_CONTACT_RESPONSE`) and opted-in pairs, frozen while the pair's island sleeps, cleared on removal | `btGhostPairCallback` fills `m_overlappingObjects` from broad-phase pairs: an AABB overlap (margin-padded bounds), no narrow-phase gate; there is no dedicated ghost type here — the sensor flag plays it | Pure exposure of the state the events already track (no second bookkeeping structure); the touch-level listing is strictly tighter than Bullet's bounds-level one, so trigger volumes report real contacts, not near misses. A per-AABB-overlap variant (a broad-phase callback) would be a separate API if a consumer ever needs the looser set |
| Rolling friction (2026-09-07) | `RigidBody.rolling_friction` installs two pure-angular torsional rows per contact point (`btPlaneSpace1` basis, per-iteration clamp `min(rf · Jn, rf)` while the contact carries a normal impulse), solved interleaved after the tangent blocks like our tangential-friction rows | `m_rollingFriction` + the global `m_tmpSolverContactRollingFrictionConstraintPool` solved after the friction pool each iteration; combined coefficient `rf0·μ1 + rf1·μ0` clamped ±10 copied verbatim (the upstream quirk — not a product) | Row math, basis and coefficient are digit-exact (parity `rolling` ball_rf exceed = 0 in f32 and f64). ## 2. Numerical differences from Bullet defaults

| Parameter | This crate | Bullet default | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restitution velocity threshold | `1.0` m/s | `m_restitutionVelocityThreshold = 0.2` | Low-speed bounces are suppressed (more stable, slightly different feel) |
| Penetration slop | `0.005` m, and the slop zone is a *dead zone*: penetration inside `[0, slop]` gets neither Baumgarte nor softening; separated (margin-band) points soften by `separation · inv_dt` exactly like Bullet's `velocityError -= penetration * invTimeStep` | `m_linearSlop = 0.0` (Baumgarte acts on full penetration; separation softening on any positive distance) | Resting bodies settle ~5 mm deeper; more stable. Bullet's slop-0 formula applied with our 0.005 slop would soften shallow resting contacts by an extra ~0.3 m/s — the dead zone keeps resting behavior unchanged from the pre-band build |
| EPA `INSIDE_EPS` / `FALLBACK` | Not implemented (degenerate → abort with current best) | `INSIDE_EPS = 0.01` (f32), `FALLBACK = 10 × EPA_ACCURACY` | Edge-case polytope handling may differ in extreme configurations |
| Warm-starting factor | `0.85` | `m_warmstartingFactor = 0.85` | Aligned |

## 3. Not yet implemented

- **CCD candidate pruning** (added 2026-09-02): each fast-mover's sweep pretests every target against the swept segment's bound box (`Aabb::new(from.min(to), from.max(to)).expanded(radius)`) before the exact cast — a conservative overlap pretest on the margin-padded proxy AABBs, so the output is unchanged and only the per-step cost differs from Bullet's broad-phase-pruned `convexSweepTest`. (Removed bodies are skipped in the sweep — their proxy is destroyed; joint-linked pairs with suppressed collisions are skipped too, like Bullet's dispatcher `needsCollision`, since 2026-09-01.)
- **Collision filter groups / masks** (ported 2026-09-02): `RigidBody::collision_group` / `collision_mask` (with `SoftBody` and `MultiBody` `LinkCollider` equivalents) implement the symmetric `btBroadphaseProxy` pair test (`a.group & b.mask != 0 && b.group & a.mask != 0`, defaults group 1 / mask all), gating every narrow-phase pair, the CCD sweep and the soft-body dispatch. Trigger volumes work through `CollisionFlags::NO_CONTACT_RESPONSE` (Bullet's `CF_NO_CONTACT_RESPONSE`): the pair is detected and queried normally but no contact rows are built, so nothing is blocked or woken. Per-ray filter masks and the custom overlap-filter callback are in (2026-09-02): rays carry their own `RayFilter` group/mask (the symmetric test, default group 1 / mask all — note the semantic change: a body whose mask excludes group 1 was previously ray-tested unconditionally and is now invisible to default rays), and `PhysicsWorld::set_pair_filter` gates rigid pairs and the CCD sweep on top of the group & mask test (soft and multibody-collider pairs do not consult it yet). Default scenes (group 1 / mask all / everything with contact response) behave identically.
- **Collision events** (added 2026-09-02, no Bullet counterpart): `world.contact_events()` drains `CollisionEvent::Started/Stopped { a, b, sensor }` pair transitions per step, Rapier-style, with a per-body opt-in (`RigidBody::collision_events`) so quiet scenes track nothing. Sleeping pairs carry their touch state (no churn); a removed body emits `Stopped` for its touching pairs. Scope: rigid-rigid pairs only — multibody-collider and soft-body pairs are not event sources yet (see §3 API surface). Sensor pairs (`NO_CONTACT_RESPONSE`) report with `sensor = true`.
- **Featherstone multibody details**: the multibody subsystem (`bullet_dynamics::multibody`, port of `BulletDynamics/Featherstone`) implements the core `btMultiBody` (all five joint types), the constraint classes (`btMultiBodyPoint2Point`, `btMultiBodyFixedConstraint`, `btMultiBodySliderConstraint`, `btMultiBodyJointMotor`, `btMultiBodyGearConstraint`, `btMultiBodyJointLimitConstraint`, `btMultiBodySphericalJointLimit`, `btMultiBodySphericalJointMotor`) and is stepped by the fused `PhysicsWorld`; link/base colliders register in the shared broad phase and solve contact rows with rigid bodies and each other (phases B–D of the multibody collision design, `docs/multibody-collision.md`), and `btMultiBodyJointFeedback` reaction reporting is ported (phase E, see §1). The multibody-vs-rigidbody constraint variants are ported for the three Bullet classes that expose them (`point2point_to_rigid` / `fixed_to_rigid` / `slider_to_rigid`; the rigid body always sits on side B and the transposed rigid-frame composition `frameInB^T · R` is reproduced verbatim); their rows follow our pre-solve gravity integration instead of Bullet's per-frame gravity leak (see §1). Not implemented: the MLCP solver, the RK4 integrator, global velocities, interpolation (predicted) transforms, kinematic links, articulated warm starting (`SOLVER_USE_ARTICULATED_WARMSTARTING` — off by default in Bullet, so the default behavior matches; the applied-impulse writeback that would feed it is in place), and link CCD (upstream has no link CCD either). Sleeping matches Bullet's motion check (`checkMotionAndSleepIfRequired`); wake-up goes through the simulation islands. Split impulse is not implemented for multibodies, exactly like Bullet.
- **Persistent-manifold extras**: point lifetime counters, friction anchors and contact-processed callbacks are not implemented (the anchored refresh, impulse inheritance and pair-lifecycle pruning are — see §1); the multibody contact path still re-detects every step (its rows assemble differently anyway).
- **Unported feature classes** (the remaining gaps by size, re-verified against the codebase 2026-09-01): `btGeneric6DofSpringConstraint` / `btGeneric6DofSpring2Constraint` (spring-driven limit/motor axes), `btUniversalConstraint`, the rigid-body `btGearConstraint` (the *multibody* gear IS ported), ghost objects (`btGhostObject` / `btPairCachingGhostObject` — the trigger-volume use case is covered by `NO_CONTACT_RESPONSE` + collision events + `contact_test`, but a ghost's persistent overlap listing is not), engine callbacks (`gContactAddedCallback`, manifold contact-processed callbacks, `btDynamicsWorld::setInternalTickCallback`; collision events cover the enter/exit use case Rapier-style), debug draw (`btIDebugDraw`), serialization (`.bullet` / `btSerializer`), motion states (`btMotionState` — poses are read directly off the bodies), and the multithreaded solver (`btThreadSupportInterface`; our planned parallel-island solve is a perf item, not a parity item). Kinematic rigid bodies (`CF_KINEMATIC_OBJECT`) ARE ported (2026-09-04, `CollisionFlags::KINEMATIC` + `RigidBody::new_kinematic`; see `api-gaps.md` P6 — the pose-driving and sleep deviations live in §1).
- **Rigid-body property surface** (all default-off in Bullet, so default behavior is identical — verified absent 2026-09-01): anisotropic friction (`m_anisotropicFriction`, consumed in the solver's contact setup), rolling friction (`m_rollingFriction`), and per-object contact stiffness/damping (`CF_HAS_CONTACT_STIFFNESS_DAMPING`). Per-body gravity IS ported (2026-09-02): `RigidBody::set_gravity` syncs from the world at `add_body` time, `PhysicsWorld::set_gravity` propagates to active bodies only (sleeping bodies keep the stale value until woken — the upstream quirk, copied verbatim), and `CollisionFlags::DISABLE_WORLD_GRAVITY` detaches a body (`BT_DISABLE_WORLD_GRAVITY`). Linear/angular damping IS ported (2026-09-02): `RigidBody::set_damping` clamps both factors to `[0, 1]` like `setDamping`, and the velocity integration applies `v *= (1 - d)^dt` before gravity/forces (`predictUnconstraintMotion` semantics; zero factors skip the `powf`, keeping the default configuration bit-identical); the opt-in "additional damping" block of `applyDamping` (`m_additionalDamping` + thresholds/factors) is not ported. Sleeping thresholds ARE exposed as public fields, matching Bullet's per-body setters.
- **Query and solver-kernel API surface**: all-hits rays and ray filter masks are in (2026-09-02: `raycast_all` / `raycast_all_filtered` / `raycast_closest_filtered` — the rigid ray walk consumes the symmetric group & mask test, see the filter note above); the soft-body solver kernel enums omit the cluster-link kernels `PSolve_CLinks` / `VSolve_CLinks` (Bullet's default presets never install them, so behavior matches — only custom sequences would need them); compound child transforms are in (2026-09-07, see below).
- **Typed body handles** (2026-09-03): `add_body` / `add_multibody` / `add_soft_body` return `BodyHandle` / `MultiBodyHandle` / `SoftBodyHandle` newtypes instead of indices — the analog of Bullet's raw `btRigidBody*` / `btMultiBody*` / `btSoftBody*` pointers. No generation counters: a handle wraps the 0-based slot, so the identity semantics match the index era exactly (stable rigid slots, `remove_soft_body` renumbers soft bodies, lookups of removed rigid bodies panic). Constraint / multibody-constraint *list* slots stay index-based (like Bullet's `btTypedObject::m_userConstraintPtr`-less list walking).
- **Shapes and toolkits**: scaled-shape wrappers (`btScaledBvhTriangleMeshShape`, `btUniformScalingShape`, `btConvex2DShape`), multi-part triangle meshes (`btTriangleIndexVertexArray` parts; one vertex/index pair here), `btShapeHull`, the Extras toolkits (`ConvexDecomposition`, `HACD`/`VHACD` — convex decomposition is expected to be done offline like Bullet's own demos suggest for stacks), the deformable soft-body system (`btDeformableMultiBodyDynamicsWorld` / NeoHookean / reduced volumes — tracked in `docs/softbody.md` §11, out of scope of the classic system), and the toy world variants `btSimpleDynamicsWorld` / `btContinuousDynamicsWorld` (deliberately not ported). None of these change the behavior of the ported pipeline; scenes that need them need the port.
- **Driving on dynamic objects** (vehicle): the suspension raycast ground is always the fixed body, like Bullet's `btRaycastVehicle` (see §1).
- **Character controller API surface**: the directional `jump(v)` variant (jump along an arbitrary axis at `|v|`), the `setInterpolateUp(false)` toggle and `setMaxJumpHeight` are not exposed. The default `interpolateUp = true` step-up interpolation is ported; `m_maxJumpHeight`'s only consumer is commented out upstream (dead code in Bullet), so nothing is lost. Defaults verified against the constructor (2026-09-01): gravity `9.8·3`, fall 55, jump 10, max slope 45°, `m_maxPenetrationDepth = 0.2`, `m_useWalkDirection = true`, damping 0, `m_addedMargin = 0.02` (the margin itself is a recorded deviation, §4).
- **Compound child transforms** (added 2026-09-07, verified against `btCompoundShape.cpp:86-135`): `Compound::set_child_transform` / `remove_child` / `child_mut` + `RigidBody::set_child_transform` / `remove_child_shape` + `PhysicsWorld::set_child_transform` / `remove_child_shape` cover `updateChildTransform` / `removeChildShape` / `updateSingleAabb`. Deliberate deviations: (a) no `m_dynamicAabbTree` — our compound computes AABB/support/inertia on demand with no caches, so the tree update branch and the `shouldRecalculateLocalAabb` flag are structurally unnecessary (always-current semantics); (b) `remove_child` keeps child order (`Vec::remove`) where Bullet's `removeChildShapeByIndex` swap-and-pops (the last child moves into the removed slot — an index-observable difference; ours is the natural semantics for usize-indexed children, Bullet's is an array implementation detail behind pointer-based lookup); (c) the inertia is recomputed automatically for dynamic bodies on both mutate and remove, where Bullet leaves the body inertia stale until the user re-calls `setMassProps` (ours cannot go stale); (d) the world layer refreshes the broad-phase proxy immediately with the same `±gContactBreakingThreshold` margin as `btCollisionWorld::updateSingleAabb` (verified verbatim, `btCollisionWorld.cpp:153-160`), so sleeping/static compounds are safe to mutate without an extra call; no `m_updateRevision` counter — nothing caches compound state across steps to invalidate; (e) our refresh skips `updateSingleAabb`'s CCD branch (`m_useContinuous && rigid && !isStaticOrKinematic` re-expands the proxy through the *interpolation* transform, `:162-167`) — we have no interpolation transform; awake dynamic proxies are re-covered by the per-step predicted-pose pass, and the bodies that actually depend on the immediate refresh (sleeping/static) are excluded from that branch upstream anyway. Kinematic compounds refresh naturally through the per-step AABB pass.

- Hinge joint: `ConstraintKind::Hinge` mirrors `btHingeConstraint` using the classic `getInfo2Internal` formulation — three linear pivot rows (skippable via `angular_only`), two angular rows keeping the hinge axes parallel (bias `−k·(ax1×ax2)·p/q` in our mirrored row convention), and an optional fifth row combining the angular limits (`btAngularLimit` with the center-limit math, bias factor 0.3, bounce via the relaxation factor) and the motor (`getMotorFactor` fade + `±maxMotorImpulse` clamp), with the row clamped `[0,+inf]`/`[−inf,0]`/unbounded by the limit kind. Deviations: the frames use a deterministic perpendicular basis around the given axes (the zero-angle reference is not aligned to body A's initial X axis), and the modern frame-offset linear-row formulation is not used; `useReferenceFrameA` is not supported (`m_referenceSign` fixed to +1).
- Fixed joint: `ConstraintKind::Fixed` mirrors `btFixedConstraint` (a `btGeneric6DofConstraint` with every limit zeroed) as three point-to-point linear rows (world axes) plus three angular rows locking the relative orientation. The angular rows use the locked-6DOF branch of the classic recipe: the Euler angles of `trA⁻¹·trB` driven to zero along the `m_calculatedAxis` axes. `matrixToEulerXYZ` is ported verbatim (including its sign conventions) and was validated against the real Bullet library: an `Rz(+θ)` misalignment is corrected with `ω_z = −k·θ`, exactly like Bullet. The linear rows use the world-axis point-to-point recipe rather than Bullet's frame-axis/frame-offset rows.
- Cone-twist joint: `ConstraintKind::ConeTwist` mirrors `btConeTwistConstraint` — three linear pivot rows (point-to-point style, `m_linERP` defaulting to the global ERP like Bullet's flag-less path), the elliptical swing limit (`computeConeLimitInfo` ellipse intersection + `adjustSwingAxisToUseEllipseNormal`), the one-sided twist limit (`computeTwistLimitInfo`, `m_limitSoftness` default 1.0, `m_biasFactor` 0.3, `m_relaxationFactor` 1.0), the locked-swing (two rows along `trA`'s Y/Z) and hinge sub-branches below `CONETWIST_DEF_FIX_THRESH` (0.05), and the motor (`setMotorTarget` clamping the target into the cone). The near-zero twist-axis guard uses Bullet's precision-dependent `SIMD_EPSILON` (normalizing a tiny axis makes the row's effective mass explode as `1/|axis|²` and the f64 build re-excites the twist). The obsolete-solver motor path (angular impulses from the target delta) is not ported — the modern getInfo2 path drives the swing row instead; CFM flags are not implemented.
- Slider joint: `ConstraintKind::Slider` mirrors `btSliderConstraint` with the frame-offset formulation (`USE_OFFSET_FOR_CONSTANT_FRAME`) — two angular ortho rows from `u = ax1A×ax1B`, two linear ortho rows using the offset-corrected lever arms (`relA/relB` through the mass-weighted `totalDist`), the blended slider axis (`normalize(factA·ax1A + factB·ax1B)`) and optional linear/angular limit and motor rows (velocity-bias correction `k·limitErr`, bounce `|1 − dampingLim|`, `getMotorFactor` motors). Bullet defaults are kept: free linear travel (`1.0 > −1.0`), locked rotation (`0 == 0`). The motor drives body B relative to body A along the axis (Bullet's convention, verified against the real library — pass the static anchor as body A). CFM flags and the obsolete-solver path are not implemented; like Bullet, the linear limit is a velocity bias, so a body pushed hard into a limit rebounds and coasts back (no friction damping by default).
- Generic 6DOF joint: `ConstraintKind::Generic6Dof` mirrors `btGeneric6DofConstraint` (the classic `getInfo1`/`getInfo2` recipe, angular rows solved first) — per-axis `btTranslationalLimitMotor`/`btRotationalLimitMotor` with limits, motors (`getMotorFactor` with the linear tag-velocity flip, `±maxMotorForce/fps` windows) and bounce. The angular rows use `m_calculatedAxis` with the Euler-angle errors (`−k·err`, `btAdjustAngleToLimits`, `testAngularLimitMotor`); the linear rows use frame A's columns with point-to-point lever arms instead of Bullet's frame-offset formulation (deviation), `+k·err`, and the mirrored one-sided windows. Bullet defaults: linear locked, angular free. CFM, `useLinearReferenceFrameA`, the ERP/CFM flag overrides and the obsolete solver are not implemented; the global ERP parameter is unused (rows use the per-axis `stop_erp` = 0.2).
- Featherstone multibody core: `bullet_dynamics::multibody::MultiBody` is a port of `btMultiBody` — the link/joint model (`btMultiBodyLink`: revolute/prismatic/spherical/planar/fixed, `m_axes` spatial joint axes, `m_zeroRotParentToThis`/`m_dVector`/`m_eVector`), forward kinematics (`forwardKinematics`, `localPosToWorld` etc.), `compTreeLinkVelocities`, Featherstone's articulated-body algorithm (`computeAccelerationsArticulatedBodyAlgorithmMultiDof`: upward/downward/upward passes, per-link `D⁻¹` with the `dof_offset²` scatter layout, gyro and Coriolis terms, `m_useGyroTerm`, damping `K1=K2=0.04` with Bullet's `safeNorm` = max-component length), the 6x6 spatial-inertia solve for the base (`solveImatrix`), the constraint-impulse response (`calcAccelerationDeltasMultiDof`, reusing the cached factorization) and the exponential-map position integrator (`stepPositionsMultiDof` with `ANGULAR_MOTION_THRESHOLD` clamping). Defaults match Bullet: `m_maxAppliedImpulse = 1000`, `m_maxCoordinateVelocity = 100`, sleeping `epsilon = 0.05`, `timeout = 2 s`. Validated against the real Bullet library (`tools/multibody_probe.cpp` vs `crates/bullet/examples/multibody_probe.rs`, 10 scenes × f32/f64): free base, motor, prismatic, planar and gear are bit-identical; pendulum, point-to-point and spherical match to print precision; the fixed-weld pair matches Bullet's constraint dynamics within ~3 cm over 5 s (both engines exhibit the same initial-kick decay); the two-link chain is exact for ~15 frames then diverges chaotically (double-pendulum sensitivity, the same class as the chaotic mesh scene).
- Multibody constraints: `bullet_dynamics::multibody::MultiBodyConstraint` mirrors `btMultiBodyConstraint` and its subclasses — per-constraint `m_data` block (impulses + jacobians + positions), `fillMultiBodyConstraint` (jacobian from `fillConstraintJacobianMultiDof` or pre-finalized, unit-impulse response, `jacDiagABInv = 1/(J·M⁻¹Jᵀ)` with `SIMD_EPSILON` singularity guard, `rhs = −penetration·erp/dt + (desiredVel − relVel)·jacDiagABInv` with `m_erp = 0.2`, no split impulse like Bullet). Point-to-point (three world-axis rows), fixed (three pivot rows + three angular rows via `matrixToEulerXYZ`), slider (two ortho linear rows + three angular rows), joint motor (one row along the link's joint DOF, `m_kp`/`m_kd`/`m_erp`/`m_rhsClamp` PD terms, `±maxMotorImpulse`), gear (`q̇_a + ratio·q̇_b` row, optional auxiliary link, `m_erp` position term). Rows are not warm-started (Bullet's `SOLVER_USE_ARTICULATED_WARMSTARTING` is off by default; the accumulated impulse is written back to the constraint each step).
- Multibody joint limit: `MultiBodyConstraint::joint_limit` mirrors `btMultiBodyJointLimitConstraint` — two unilateral rows on the joint's single DOF (row 0 = lower bound `+1` on body A, row 1 = upper bound `−1` on body B, the same body's parent link), created only while the joint is at or past the bound, with the classic limit RHS (`positionalError = −penetration·erp/dt`, `velocityError = −relVel`). Like Bullet's default solver info, a violation deeper than `m_splitImpulsePenetrationThreshold` (−0.04) drops the positional term (Bullet routes it into `m_rhsPenetration`, which the multibody solver ignores — split impulse is not implemented for Featherstone). Feedback normals come from the world joint axis (revolute: `m_axes[0].m_topVec`, prismatic: `m_bottomVec`), matching Bullet.
- Multibody spherical limit: `MultiBodyConstraint::spherical_joint_limit` mirrors `btMultiBodySphericalJointLimit` — the joint quaternion is split into its cone (swing) and twist parts (`shortestArcQuat` on the rotated Z axis), the swing/twist angles are clamped to the `swingx`/`swingy`/`twist` ranges, and up to three unilateral pure-angular rows along the link frame's world columns push the joint back inside (one-sided windows, `kp` position gain default 0.2, per-DOF gains/impulse limits via `m_use_multi_dof_params`). Rows are skipped while `|posError| ≤ SIMD_EPSILON` (precision-dependent, like Bullet). Validated against the real library (`spherical_limit` probe scene, print precision).
- Multibody spherical motor: `MultiBodyConstraint::spherical_joint_motor` mirrors `btMultiBodySphericalJointMotor` — three bilateral pure-angular rows along the link frame's world columns drive the joint orientation toward `desired_position` (default identity) from the Euler angle difference (`matrixToEulerXYZ`), each damped by `m_damping[i]` (default 1) and clamped to `±maxMotorImpulse`. Validated against the real library (`spherical_motor` probe scene, print precision).
- Multibody solver + world: `MultiBodyWorld` mirrors `btMultiBodyDynamicsWorld` for the multibody-only subset — gravity per base/link, forward kinematics, velocity integration through the ABA, row conversion, projected Gauss-Seidel over the non-contact pool (10 outer × 1 inner iterations, alternating direction by iteration parity like `solveSingleIteration`), writeback (`applyDeltaVeeMultiDof` with the `±maxCoordinateVelocity` clamp, applied impulses stored on the constraints), `processDeltaVeeMultiDof2` flush, position integration with `addSplitV`/`substractSplitV` (no-op without split impulses), `checkMotionAndSleepIfRequired` per body, and constraint-linked wake-up in place of the island merge. Stepping order matches Bullet: gravity → `solveExternalForces` (ABA) → `solveInternalConstraints` (rows + PGS + writeback) → `integrateTransforms` (positions; sleeping bodies have velocities cleared) → force clearing + sleep check.

- **Scalar math routing**: every engine-side transcendental
  (`sqrt/sin/cos/atan2/asin/acos/powf`, ~64 call sites) goes through
  `bullet_math::math`. The `std` branch is an inlined pass-through to the
  standard method — default builds stay bit-identical (parity
  `composite_consumer` + `falling_box` re-recorded digit-for-digit
  against the ledgers, perf stack_1000/rain_500 inside the noise band).
  `libm` builds route to the pure-Rust libm crate, which is musl-derived:
  expect last-ulp differences from the platform CRT on the transcendental
  results (glam's internal math follows the same split via
  `glam/std` vs `glam/nostd-libm`). The ledgers are recorded on std
  builds and remain authoritative.
- **EPA scratch pooling**: `EpaScratch` (vertex/face/boundary buffers) is
  caller-owned: `PhysicsWorld` holds one and threads it through the
  narrow-phase detection calls, so steady-state passes allocate nothing
  on any platform. The former `thread_local!` pool (std-only, one pool
  per thread shared by every world) became a per-world pool with the
  same no-allocation steady state and no TLS requirement.
- **Step timings**: `StepTimings` defaults to the std monotonic `Instant`
  (process-relative `OnceLock` start; `Instant` arithmetic is i64
  nanoseconds, so the derived spans are identical to direct diffs).
  Without `std` the default clock reads zero; hosts inject their
  platform clock via `PhysicsWorld::set_step_clock(fn() -> Duration)`.
  Injected clocks should be monotonic: a backwards read saturates the
  span to zero (never panics, never negative), the clock is only read
  while timing is enabled (~8 reads/step), and mid-step clock swaps
  resynchronize on the next full step. A bevy_platform-style unsafe
  rdtsc/asm fallback was evaluated and rejected (zero-unsafe policy,
  TSC cross-core sync/frequency caveats).
- **`vec!`/`Vec`/`Box`/`BTreeSet`**: lib code reaches them through
  `#[macro_use] extern crate alloc` plus per-module `use alloc::...`
  imports; `std` builds resolve the identical std types. Verified:
  `cargo check -p bullet --no-default-features --features libm,f32|f64`
  and a `wasm32-unknown-unknown` cross-check, zero errors/warnings.
